Proof of Attendance for Online AA Meetings

Online meetings make a court-ordered requirement easier to keep: there are meetings every hour of the day. The hard part is proof. There’s no chair to hand a paper card to on Zoom. Here’s how to get a record your probation officer can trust.

First: check that online meetings count

Many courts and probation officers accept online meetings, but not all, and some cap how many of your required meetings can be online. Check your court order or ask your probation officer before you rely on them.

Three ways to prove you attended

1. Ask the host in the chat

After the meeting, message the host or secretary privately and ask them to confirm your attendance. Some online groups have a secretary who emails confirmations; their website usually says how to request one. Keep every reply.

2. Keep your own record as backup

Note the meeting name, group, date, start and end time, and the host’s name. A screenshot with your own name and the time can help, but don’t capture other people’s faces or full names: anonymity matters in these rooms.

3. Have the host confirm a link Best

Log the meeting in MyRecoveryPal and paste its confirmation link in the chat. The host opens it on their own device, enters their name, and the meeting is marked chair-confirmed with the time on your attendance report. You can’t confirm your own meetings, and editing an entry afterwards removes the confirmation.

Tips that make hosts say yes

  • Use your real first name and last initial as your Zoom display name.
  • Keep your camera on if you can. Some hosts and courts expect it.
  • Stay for the whole meeting, then ask. Hosts are volunteers; a short, polite message works best.

Frequently asked questions

Do online AA meetings count for court-ordered attendance?

Many courts and probation officers accept online meetings, but not all, and some limit how many can be online. Check your court order or ask your probation officer before you rely on online meetings.

How do I get proof of attendance at a Zoom AA meeting?

After the meeting, message the host or secretary in the chat and ask them to confirm your attendance. Some online groups have a secretary who emails attendance confirmations; check the group’s website. You can also log the meeting in MyRecoveryPal and paste its confirmation link in the chat, so the host confirms it on their own device and the meeting is marked chair-confirmed on your report.

Is a screenshot of the Zoom participant list enough proof?

A screenshot showing your name, the meeting and the time is useful backup, but it is easy to fake, so many probation officers want a confirmation from the host as well. Never share screenshots of other participants’ faces or full names: anonymity is a core tradition of these meetings.

Do I need my camera on?

Some courts and some hosts expect your camera on for the whole meeting before they will confirm attendance. If yours does, keep your camera on and use your real first name as your display name.
