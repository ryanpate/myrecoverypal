How to Prove Meeting Attendance to Your Probation Officer

If your sentence or probation includes AA, NA or other recovery meetings, your probation officer will want proof. Here are your options, what each one gives your officer, and how the apps built for this compare.

Start with one question to your officer

Courts and probation offices differ, so ask your officer before you pick a method:

  • Do you accept a digital report, or only a signed paper card or your own form?
  • Do online meetings count, and is there a limit on how many?
  • Which programs count: AA and NA only, or also SMART Recovery and other secular meetings?
  • How often do you want proof, and how should I send it?

What a record your officer trusts looks like

Whatever method you use, a good record lists every meeting with its name, date, time and place, shows a running total against your requirement (for example, 3 a week), and gives your officer a reason to believe it: a signature, a confirmation from the meeting’s chair or host, or a way to check the document hasn’t been edited.

Your options

A paper court card signed by the chair

The traditional method. Bring your court’s card or a printable attendance sheet and ask the chair or secretary to sign it before the meeting starts. Each AA group decides whether to sign. Cards are free, but they get lost and signatures are hard for an officer to check.

A confirmation from an online meeting’s host

For Zoom meetings, message the host or secretary in the chat after the meeting and ask them to confirm your attendance. Some groups email confirmations. More on proof for online meetings.

An app that records each meeting

Several apps are built for court-ordered attendance. They differ in how they confirm you were there, what your officer receives, and what they cost. The table below compares them.

Which one fits your situation

  • Your officer wants GPS proof you were at the meeting location: My Meeting Card is built around GPS check-in. MyRecoveryPal doesn’t track your location; it relies on the chair confirming you instead.
  • You only attend online and want the meeting provider to vouch for you: 12 Step Online verifies attendance at its own meetings against chat logs.
  • Your requirement is Gamblers Anonymous or gambling recovery: Cope Compass is built for gambling recovery.
  • You mix in-person and online meetings, use a non-12-step program, or want to log for free and pay only when your officer asks for a report: MyRecoveryPal.
  • Your court issues its own form that must be signed in ink: use that form. Any app is a backup, not a replacement.

Frequently asked questions

How do I prove AA meeting attendance to my probation officer?

Ask your probation officer what proof they accept first. The usual options are a paper court card signed by the meeting chair, a written confirmation from an online meeting’s host, or an app that records each meeting and produces a report. Whatever you use, the record should list each meeting’s name, date, time and place, and show who confirmed it.

Will my probation officer accept an app instead of a signed court card?

Many probation officers accept digital records, but not all, and some courts issue their own form that must be signed. Show your officer a sample report before you rely on any app, and keep a paper card as backup if they are unsure.

What is the best app for court-ordered meeting attendance?

It depends on what your court asks for. My Meeting Card uses GPS check-in, 12 Step Online verifies its own online meetings against chat logs, Cope Compass focuses on gambling recovery, MeetingMaker records check-in and check-out times, and MyRecoveryPal logs in-person and online meetings of any program for free, gets each one confirmed by the chair, and produces a PDF report with a fingerprint the officer can verify online.

Can my probation officer tell if an attendance report was altered?

Only if the report is built for it. A MyRecoveryPal report carries a SHA-256 fingerprint the officer can check at myrecoverypal.com/verify/court/; any edit to the PDF changes the fingerprint. Cope Compass signs its records, and 12 Step Online will confirm a document on request from a legal official. A plain screenshot or spreadsheet offers no such check.

Does an AA group have to sign my court card?

No. Each AA group decides for itself whether to sign court slips. Ask the chair or secretary before the meeting starts. If a group won’t sign, an app confirmation from the chair, or a different meeting, are the usual alternatives.

Do online meetings count for court-ordered attendance?

Many courts accept online meetings, but some require in-person attendance or cap how many can be online. Check your court order or ask your probation officer.
