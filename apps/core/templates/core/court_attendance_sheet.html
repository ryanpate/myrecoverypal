How to use it

  1. Fill in the top with your name, case number, court and how many meetings you need (for example, 3 per week for 90 days).
  2. Bring it to every meeting. Afterwards, ask the chair or secretary to fill in one row and sign. Most groups are used to this.
  3. Photograph it after each meeting. If the sheet is lost, the photos are your backup.
  4. Turn it in the way your probation officer asks, and keep a copy.

What probation officers look for

An attendance record is only useful if your probation officer can trust it. They usually want to see which meetings you attended (name, date, time, place), a running total against what your order requires, and a confirmation from someone at the meeting. Requirements differ between courts, so check your court order or ask your PO which format they accept. If your court gave you its own form, use that one.

Frequently asked questions

Who signs an AA meeting attendance sheet?

Usually the meeting chair or secretary. Arrive a few minutes early or stay after the meeting and ask them to print their name, sign, and add a group phone number or contact. Some groups use a stamp or initials instead; your probation officer decides what they accept.

Will my court accept this attendance sheet?

Many courts and probation officers accept any sheet that shows the date, meeting, location and a chair’s signature, but some issue their own form. Check your court order or ask your probation officer before you rely on it. If your court gave you a form, use theirs.

How do I get an attendance sheet signed at an online meeting?

There is no one to hand a paper sheet to on Zoom. Message the host in the meeting chat after the meeting and ask them to confirm your attendance, or log the meeting in MyRecoveryPal and paste its confirmation link in the chat so the host can confirm it on their own device. More on proof of attendance for online meetings.

What if I lose my attendance sheet?

A lost paper sheet usually means those meetings can’t be proven. Take a photo of the sheet after every meeting, or log meetings digitally so the record can’t be lost.
