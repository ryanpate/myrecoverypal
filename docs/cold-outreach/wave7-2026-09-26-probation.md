```
Hi Jamie,

Following up on my note from September 10, because two things have changed that answer the first question anyone asks about attendance records: how do you know they're real?

1. Chair-confirmed attendance. At the meeting, the participant shows a one-time QR code and the chair or secretary confirms on their own phone. The report marks that meeting "Chair-confirmed" with the chair's name and the time, and everything else stays labeled "Self-reported". A participant can't confirm their own attendance, and editing an entry afterwards removes its confirmation.

2. Cost. Logging meetings is now free for participants. A verified PDF report is $9.99 one-time, and there's still nothing to buy or set up on the court's side.

The one-page officer guide covers both:

## Part B — wave 7: 15 Indiana county program directors (new)

### Tippecanoe County — David
**To:** dhullinger@tippecanoe.in.gov
**Subject:** How does Tippecanoe County verify AA/NA attendance cards?

```
Hi David,

Quick question: when someone in the Tippecanoe County Court Services program brings your staff a paper AA or NA sign-in card, how do you know it's real?

I'm Ryan, founder of MyRecoveryPal, and we built a way to make that checkable. Participants log each meeting in the app, and at the meeting the chair or secretary can confirm it by scanning a one-time QR code on their own phone. When your program needs proof, the participant generates a PDF report that labels every meeting "Chair-confirmed" or "Self-reported". The report also carries a fingerprint your staff can check on our site in about 30 seconds, with no account. The check confirms only that the document is genuine and unaltered; it shows no names or case details.

It covers AA, NA, CA, SMART Recovery, Refuge, LifeRing and secular meetings, so it fits OWI and drug-offense conditions without favoring one program.

One page for officers:
https://www.myrecoverypal.com/for-probation-officers/

Logging is free for participants, and a verified report is $9.99 one-time. There's nothing for the court to buy, sign or install.

Would a report like this be acceptable documentation for your program? And if not, what would it need first? A "no, because..." is genuinely useful to me.
