## Post 5: What a probation officer looks for (Mon Dec 7)

**Instagram**

What a probation officer usually wants from an attendance record:

1. Which meetings you went to. Name, date, time, place.

2. A running total against what your order requires.

3. A reason to trust it. That is the chair's signature or confirmation.

A paper sheet can cover all three if every row is filled in and signed.

Requirements differ from court to court. Read your order or ask your PO which format they take, and if they gave you a form, use it.

## Post 6: Proof at an online meeting (Wed Dec 9)

**Instagram**

There is no one to hand a paper sheet to on Zoom.

If you attend meetings online for a court requirement, do this:

First, check that online meetings count. Many courts and probation officers accept them. Not all do, and some cap how many.

Then, after the meeting, message the host privately and ask them to confirm you attended. Keep every reply.

Use your real first name and last initial as your display name. Stay for the whole meeting before you ask. Hosts are volunteers, so keep the message short.
